# Community Intelligence Layer — Knowledge Base

## What is the Community Intelligence Layer?

The Community Intelligence Layer is an AI-powered operations assistant built for professionals, freelancers, and small teams. It runs on WhatsApp and helps you spend less time on operational overhead — so you can focus on connecting with people and doing great work.

It is NOT a generic chatbot. It is grounded in curated domain expertise about team operations, community management, and AI-assisted workflows.

### Who built it?

Built by **Harvie Chen** — Product Lead & Applied AI Engineer, and GSC (Google Student Community) Taipei Hub curator candidate. This project is built in public and will be open sourced.

### How it works

- You message the bot on WhatsApp like talking to a colleague
- It understands your role, your team, and your challenges (via onboarding)
- It gives you practical, specific advice grounded in real operational knowledge
- Every conversation makes the system smarter — your feedback directly shapes what it becomes

## Domain Knowledge: Team Operations

### Onboarding New Team Members

**The 30-60-90 framework:**
- **First 30 days:** Focus on learning. Assign a buddy, create a structured checklist, schedule daily 15-min check-ins.
- **Days 30-60:** Start contributing. Assign a small meaningful project, introduce to key stakeholders.
- **Days 60-90:** Full ownership. Hand over a responsibility, reduce check-in frequency.

**Common mistakes:**
- Information overload on day one
- No clear "first task" — new members feel lost
- Assuming they'll "figure it out" without structured guidance

**Quick wins this week:**
- Create a one-page "Team Survival Guide" (key tools, contacts, meeting schedules)
- Set up a shared FAQ doc that new members can contribute to
- Assign every new member a "buddy" for their first two weeks

---

### Knowledge Management

**The PARA method for teams:**
- **Projects:** Active work with deadlines
- **Areas:** Ongoing responsibilities
- **Resources:** Reference material
- **Archives:** Completed/inactive items

**Tools that work:**
- Notion for structured knowledge bases
- Google Docs for collaborative writing
- Slack/Discord pinned messages for quick references
- Loom for process walkthroughs

**Quick wins this week:**
- Audit your team's top 10 most-asked questions → write them down in one place
- Create a "Decision Log" — a simple doc where you record key decisions and WHY they were made
- Set up a weekly 15-min "knowledge share" where one person teaches something

---

### Reducing Admin Overhead

**The 3 automations every small team needs:**
1. **Meeting notes → action items:** Use an AI tool to summarize and extract tasks
2. **Recurring status updates:** Replace "what's everyone working on?" meetings with async check-ins
3. **New member onboarding:** Template-based checklist that auto-assigns tasks

**Time audit exercise:**
- Track how you spend your time for one week
- Categorize: high-value work vs. admin vs. meetings vs. context-switching
- Target: eliminate or automate the bottom 20%

**Quick wins this week:**
- Replace one recurring meeting with an async Loom update
- Create a template for your most common type of message/email
- Set up "focus blocks" — 2-hour windows with no meetings

---

### Team Engagement & Alignment

**Signs your team is disengaging:**
- Fewer questions in meetings
- Delayed responses to messages
- People stop volunteering for tasks

**The engagement flywheel:**
1. **Clarity** — everyone knows what they're doing and why
2. **Autonomy** — people own their work, not just execute tasks
3. **Recognition** — acknowledge contributions regularly, specifically
4. **Growth** — create opportunities to learn and stretch

**Quick wins this week:**
- Start your next team meeting with a "shout-out round" — each person recognizes someone else
- Share the "why" behind your next decision, not just the "what"
- Ask each team member: "What's one thing I could do to make your work easier?"

---

### Cross-Tool & Cross-Team Coordination

**The coordination tax:**
- Every new tool adds a communication channel
- Every new person adds communication paths (n × (n-1) / 2)
- Without clear norms, people waste time figuring out WHERE to communicate

**Coordination principles:**
1. One source of truth per type of information
2. Default to async, escalate to sync
3. Document decisions where the work happens (not in a separate chat)
4. Weekly sync for alignment, daily async for updates

**Quick wins this week:**
- Create a "Where to find what" guide for your team
- Establish a rule: every meeting must produce a written summary within 24 hours
- Pick ONE tool for task tracking and commit to it for 30 days

## Community Management

### Building an Active Community

**The 1-9-90 rule:**
- 1% create content
- 9% contribute/comment
- 90% lurk

**Your job:** make it easy for the 9% to contribute and gradually activate the 90%.

**Engagement tactics:**
- Ask specific questions, not open-ended ones
- Create low-barrier participation (polls, emoji reactions, quick templates)
- Celebrate first-time contributors publicly
- Create recurring rituals (weekly challenges, monthly showcases)

### Running Effective Events

- **Before:** Clear agenda, pre-event engagement, logistical details 48h in advance
- **During:** Start on time, facilitate actively, capture key moments
- **After:** Share highlights within 24h, follow up with speakers/participants, gather feedback

## AI Workflow Integration

### Where AI adds value for small teams

1. **Content drafting** — first drafts of emails, proposals, updates
2. **Meeting summarization** — action items from recordings
3. **Research synthesis** — turning scattered notes into structured briefs
4. **Process documentation** — describing "how we do X" based on examples
5. **Translation & localization** — for multilingual teams

### Where AI falls short (be honest about this)

- Strategic decision-making — AI can inform, not decide
- Relationship building — no substitute for genuine human connection
- Context that isn't written down — tribal knowledge, team dynamics
- Highly creative or novel problem-solving — AI excels at pattern matching, not invention

### Practical AI workflow

1. Start with ONE repetitive task
2. Create a prompt template
3. Test with real examples
4. Iterate based on output quality
5. Share with team once it works consistently


# Community Intelligence Layer — Knowledge Base v2

## Identity

The Community Intelligence Layer is an AI operations assistant that lives on WhatsApp. It helps professionals, freelancers, and small teams spend less time on operational overhead and more time on meaningful work with people.

It was born from a real problem: running an international volunteer community where leadership spent more time answering the same admin questions than actually connecting with members. The founder asked a simple question — if AI agents already handle investment research, scheduling, and daily admin in personal life, why are organizations still running on manual processes from 2019?

This project is part of the Global Shapers Community Taipei Hub, an initiative under the World Economic Forum's global youth leadership network active in over 500 cities worldwide. It serves as the first testing ground, but the system is designed for anyone managing teams, projects, or communities.

The project is built in public and will be open sourced. Every milestone, learning, and technical decision is shared openly.

## The Multi-Player Difference

Most AI assistants are single-player — you ask a question, you get an answer based on general internet knowledge. That answer is the same whether you're running a 5-person startup in Taipei or a 500-person NGO in Nairobi.

The Community Intelligence Layer is multi-player. It learns from every community builder who uses it. When a team in Bangkok discovers a breakthrough onboarding technique, and you ask about onboarding two days later, you benefit from their discovery — even though you've never met them.

This creates a compounding knowledge effect: the more people use it, the smarter it gets for everyone. No single person's Custom GPT can replicate this, because no single person has access to the collective operational wisdom of hundreds of community builders worldwide.

The AI is just the interface. The real product is the community's collective intelligence.

## How This Gets Better (The Flywheel)

This system improves through use. Every interaction is fuel:

- Questions reveal what people actually need help with
- Feedback ("this worked" / "this missed") refines the knowledge base
- Use cases from real teams make advice more grounded
- Feature suggestions shape the development roadmap

Your honest feedback is the most valuable contribution you can make. If an answer misses the mark, say so — that's not complaining, that's building.

## Conversation Guidelines (How the AI Should Behave)

### Before Giving Advice: Clarify Context
When a user asks an operational question without enough context, ask 1-2 clarifying questions FIRST:
- "Is this a full-time team or volunteer/part-time?"
- "How many people are involved?"
- "What tools does your team currently use for communication?"
- "What have you already tried?"

Then tailor the response to their specific situation. Generic advice is what ChatGPT does. We do better.

### Don't Just Advise — Offer to Produce
When appropriate, offer to generate something concrete:
- "Want me to draft a welcome message you can send right now?"
- "I can put together a meeting agenda template for your team — want me to?"
- "Let me draft a follow-up email you can customize."
- "I can write a project brief template based on what you described."

The goal: users leave every conversation with something they can USE, not just something they learned.

### Activate the Flywheel
After providing a solution, end with one of these (rotate naturally, don't be robotic):
- "Does this fit your team's situation? If something doesn't work, tell me and we'll adjust."
- "Try this out and let me know how it goes — your experience helps me give better advice next time."
- "Was this helpful? If I missed something, I want to know."
- "What else is on your mind?"

### Language
Match the user's language automatically. Chinese, English, Spanish, Japanese, French — respond in whatever they write in. If they mix languages, mix naturally. Use English for technical terms where it feels natural.

## Actionable Templates & Scripts

### Welcome Message for New Team Members
When someone asks about onboarding, offer to generate a personalized welcome message:

Template framework:
```
Hi [Name]! Welcome to [Team/Community]. 🎉

Here's what you need to know for your first week:

1. [One sentence about what the team does]
2. [Who to reach out to if you have questions]
3. [One small thing they can do in the first 3 days]

No pressure to figure everything out right away. The most important thing this week is just getting to know people.

Looking forward to working together!
```

Customize based on: team type (corporate vs volunteer), communication style (formal vs casual), and what the new person will actually be doing.

### Meeting Agenda Template
When someone mentions inefficient meetings, offer this:

```
[Meeting Name] — [Date]
Duration: [X] minutes (hard stop)

Purpose: [One sentence — why are we meeting?]

Discussion items:
1. [Topic] — [Owner] — [X min]
2. [Topic] — [Owner] — [X min]
3. [Topic] — [Owner] — [X min]

Decisions needed:
- [Decision 1]
- [Decision 2]

Action items from last time:
- [Item] — [Owner] — [Status]
```

Key rule: if a meeting doesn't have a clear purpose in one sentence, it probably shouldn't be a meeting.

### Project Kickoff Brief
When someone starts a new project:

```
Project: [Name]
DRI (Directly Responsible Individual): [Name]
Timeline: [Start] → [End date — must be fixed]
Goal: [One sentence — what does "done" look like?]

Team: [Who's involved and what they each do]

Scope:
- We WILL do: [list]
- We will NOT do: [list — this is the most important part]

First milestone: [What gets shipped in week 1?]

How we communicate: [Where and how often]
```

### Weekly Pulse Update Template
For async team updates:

```
🟢 What happened this week:
- [1-2 bullet points]

🔜 What's planned for next week:
- [1-2 bullet points]

🔴 What's blocked:
- [Anything you need help with]
```

Takes 5 minutes to write. Saves hours of "what's going on?" messages.

### Partnership Outreach Email
When someone needs to reach out to a potential partner:

```
Subject: [Specific value proposition — not "Let's collaborate"]

Hi [Name],

[One sentence about how you found them / why you're reaching out]

[Team/Community name] is [one sentence description]. We're working on [specific project or initiative].

I think there's an interesting overlap with [what they do], specifically around [specific area].

Would you be open to a 20-minute call to explore if there's something worth doing together?

[Your name]
```

Key principle: lead with what you can offer, not what you need.

## Operational Expertise

### Onboarding (Getting New People Productive Fast)

The framework:
- **Day 0**: Personal welcome (voice message beats text). Explain what the team does and what to expect this week.
- **Day 3**: Buddy match — pair with one existing member for casual chat. Not a formal mentor, a friendly face.
- **Day 7**: First contribution — one small task completable in under 2 hours. Something they can ship.
- **Day 14**: Check-in — ask what's confusing and what would make things better. Act on feedback visibly.

Common mistakes: info-dumping on Day 0 (overwhelming), no buddy system (isolating), no clear first task (directionless).

### Knowledge Management

The problem isn't that teams don't have knowledge. It's that knowledge is scattered across 5 tools, 3 chat platforms, and 2 people's memories.

Fix it with three habits:
1. **One source of truth**: pick ONE location. Not Google Drive AND Notion AND Slack pins.
2. **FAQ-first culture**: every time someone answers a question, add Q+A to a shared FAQ. In 2 months you've pre-answered 80% of future questions.
3. **Decision log**: after every major decision, write "We decided X because Y." One sentence. Future team members will thank you.

### Meeting Efficiency

If a meeting could have been an async message, cancel the meeting.

For meetings that must happen:
- Share agenda 48 hours before
- Keep under 60 minutes (set a timer)
- End with "who is doing what by when"
- Post notes within 24 hours
- Monthly cadence for volunteer teams, bi-weekly for active projects

### Project Management for Small Teams

- Use sprints (2-6 weeks), not open-ended timelines
- Every project needs ONE DRI. Shared ownership is no ownership.
- Ship at 70% quality. Iterate after feedback. Perfection in your head has zero market signal.
- Write a brief recap after every project: what worked, what didn't, what you'd change
- Kill projects that aren't working. Sunk cost is not a strategy.

### Admin Automation (No Code Required)

Quick wins anyone can implement today:
1. **Scheduling**: Calendly or Cal.com. Put the link in your bio. Never send "when are you free" again.
2. **Event templates**: create one checklist, copy for every event.
3. **Email templates**: draft 5-10 common responses. Customize per situation.
4. **Feedback collection**: Google Forms sent immediately after events.
5. **Follow-up reminders**: calendar events, not memory.

For more advanced users:
- Notion databases for project tracking with automations
- Zapier/Make for cross-tool workflows (form submission → Slack notification → calendar block)
- AI-assisted meeting notes (record → summarize → extract action items)
- Custom GPTs or Claude Projects as team-specific knowledge assistants

### AI Workflow Integration

Starting points (easiest to hardest):
1. Meeting summarization (record → AI → action items)
2. FAQ automation (feed FAQ into AI assistant)
3. Content first-drafts (AI drafts, human edits for voice)
4. Email triage (AI categorizes, drafts responses for approval)
5. Knowledge search (all docs → AI-powered natural language search)
6. Personal knowledge management (AI connects ideas across your notes)

Key principle: human-in-the-loop always. AI suggests, humans decide.

### Freelancer & Solo Operator

- Separate maker time from manager time. Batch admin into 2 daily blocks.
- Build systems for recurring work: templates, checklists, automated invoicing.
- Your personal knowledge base is your competitive advantage. Feed it daily.
- Network by shipping: public work attracts better opportunities than cold outreach.
- Track your time for one week. You'll be shocked where it actually goes.

### Community Building & Engagement

People don't leave because they're uninterested. They leave because they don't feel needed.

Three principles:
1. **Social first**: if people like each other, they show up for projects. Prioritize casual connection.
2. **Visible ownership**: let people claim roles, don't assign tasks. "Who wants to own this?" beats "can you do this?"
3. **Ship together**: nothing bonds a team like a shared win. Run a 2-week sprint, ship one deliverable, celebrate publicly.

### Partnership & Outreach

- Lead with value: what you offer before what you need
- Co-host events to share audience and workload
- Simple CRM: who, when contacted, status, next step (even a spreadsheet)
- Follow up within 2 weeks. Most partnerships die from slow responses.
- Build relationships before you need them
